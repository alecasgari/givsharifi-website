Acromegaly: Diagnosis Pituitary Surgery and Long-Term Care

7 September 2026 · 8 min read · Prof. Guive Sharifi

Acromegaly is a rare hormonal disorder caused by excess growth hormone, often due to a pituitary macroadenoma. Early diagnosis and expert pituitary surgery can improve outcomes. Long-term care involves endocrinology team support and monitoring.

Share this post

Acromegaly is a rare but serious condition caused by the overproduction of growth hormone, usually due to a benign tumor called a pituitary macroadenoma. This excess hormone leads to abnormal growth of bones and tissues, causing noticeable changes in facial features, hands, and feet. Early diagnosis and treatment are crucial to prevent complications such as diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease. Patients with acromegaly often require a multidisciplinary approach involving neurosurgeons, endocrinologists, and other specialists to manage the condition effectively.

The diagnosis of acromegaly typically involves clinical evaluation, hormone testing, and imaging studies. Blood tests measure elevated levels of growth hormone and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the pituitary gland helps identify the size and location of the tumor. Once diagnosed, treatment options include surgery, medication, and sometimes radiation therapy. Pituitary surgery, especially endoscopic approaches, is often the first-line treatment to remove the tumor and reduce hormone levels.

Understanding Acromegaly and Its Causes

Acromegaly develops when the pituitary gland produces too much growth hormone after the growth plates have closed. The most common cause is a pituitary macroadenoma, a benign tumor larger than 10 millimeters. This tumor presses on surrounding tissues and secretes excess hormone, leading to the characteristic symptoms of acromegaly. The condition progresses slowly, and symptoms may be subtle at first, making early detection challenging.

Besides the tumor itself, acromegaly can cause systemic effects such as joint pain, sleep apnea, and cardiovascular problems. Because the pituitary gland controls multiple hormones, patients may also experience hormonal imbalances affecting thyroid, adrenal, and reproductive functions. A comprehensive evaluation by an endocrinology team is essential to assess the full impact of the disease and plan appropriate treatment.

Diagnosis: Clinical and Laboratory Evaluation

The clinical diagnosis of acromegaly begins with recognizing physical changes such as enlarged hands and feet, coarse facial features, and skin thickening. Patients may report symptoms like headaches, vision changes, or excessive sweating. A detailed medical history and physical examination guide further testing. Blood tests confirm elevated growth hormone and IGF-1 levels, which are key markers of the disease.

Dynamic testing, such as the oral glucose tolerance test, helps confirm the diagnosis by showing failure of growth hormone suppression. Imaging with MRI provides detailed visualization of the pituitary tumor, its size, and its relationship to nearby structures like the optic chiasm. This information is critical for surgical planning and assessing the risk of vision problems.

Pituitary Surgery for Acromegaly: Techniques and Goals

Surgical removal of the pituitary macroadenoma is the primary treatment for acromegaly. The goal is to remove as much of the tumor as possible to normalize growth hormone levels and relieve pressure on surrounding tissues. Endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery is the preferred technique, offering a minimally invasive approach through the nasal passages. This method reduces recovery time and surgical risks compared to traditional open surgery.

Your neurosurgeon will carefully assess the tumor size, location, and involvement of critical structures before surgery. Complete tumor removal may not always be possible, especially if the tumor invades nearby areas. In such cases, surgery aims to reduce tumor burden and improve symptoms, with additional treatments like medication or radiation considered afterward.

Postoperative Care and Long-Term Management

After pituitary surgery, patients require close monitoring to evaluate hormone levels and detect any residual tumor. Regular follow-up with the endocrinology team is essential to manage hormone replacement if needed and monitor for recurrence. Imaging studies such as MRI are repeated periodically to ensure the tumor has not regrown.

Long-term care also involves managing complications of acromegaly, including cardiovascular risk factors, diabetes, and joint issues. Lifestyle modifications, medication adherence, and routine health screenings play important roles in maintaining quality of life. Your healthcare team will tailor a personalized plan to support your recovery and ongoing health.

Role of the Endocrinology Team in Acromegaly Treatment

The endocrinology team plays a vital role in diagnosing and managing acromegaly. They interpret hormone test results, coordinate dynamic testing, and help optimize medical therapy when surgery alone is insufficient. Medications such as somatostatin analogs or growth hormone receptor antagonists can reduce hormone levels and control symptoms.

Endocrinologists also monitor for and treat complications related to hormone imbalances, ensuring comprehensive care. Their collaboration with neurosurgeons and radiologists ensures that each patient receives a tailored treatment plan that addresses both the tumor and systemic effects of acromegaly.

Common Symptoms and Signs of Acromegaly

  • Enlarged hands and feet with increased shoe and ring size
  • Coarse facial features including enlarged nose, lips, and jaw
  • Joint pain and stiffness
  • Excessive sweating and oily skin
  • Headaches and vision disturbances
  • Fatigue and muscle weakness
  • Sleep apnea and snoring
  • Changes in voice and dental spacing

Recognizing these symptoms early can lead to prompt diagnosis and treatment, reducing the risk of serious complications. If you notice these changes, consult your healthcare provider for evaluation.

Frequently Asked Questions About Acromegaly Pituitary Treatment

What causes acromegaly and how is it diagnosed?

Acromegaly is caused by excess growth hormone production, usually from a benign pituitary macroadenoma. Diagnosis involves clinical evaluation, blood tests measuring growth hormone and IGF-1, and MRI imaging to locate the tumor. Early diagnosis is important to prevent complications.

What are the treatment options for acromegaly?

Treatment typically starts with pituitary surgery to remove the tumor. If surgery is incomplete or not possible, medications like somatostatin analogs or radiation therapy may be used to control hormone levels and symptoms. A multidisciplinary team guides the treatment plan.

What is endoscopic pituitary surgery?

Endoscopic pituitary surgery is a minimally invasive technique performed through the nasal passages using an endoscope. It allows the surgeon to remove the tumor with less trauma, shorter recovery time, and fewer complications compared to traditional open surgery.

How is long-term care managed after surgery?

Long-term care involves regular follow-up with endocrinologists to monitor hormone levels and detect tumor recurrence. Patients may need hormone replacement therapy and management of complications such as diabetes or cardiovascular issues. Lifestyle changes and routine health checks are important.

When should I see a neurosurgeon for acromegaly?

You should consult a neurosurgeon if you have symptoms suggestive of acromegaly or if imaging shows a pituitary tumor. Early surgical evaluation helps plan the best treatment approach and improve outcomes. Your primary care doctor or endocrinologist can provide a referral.
