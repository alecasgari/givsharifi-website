Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I need to see a neurosurgeon for a brain tumour?

You should consult a neurosurgeon if you have received a diagnosis of a brain lesion or if you are experiencing persistent neurological symptoms. Common warning signs include recurring headaches, unexplained seizures, changes in vision, or sudden weakness in limbs. Your primary care physician or a neurologist will typically refer you for specialized evaluation if imaging studies reveal an abnormality. Prof. Guive Sharifi can assess your specific symptoms and imaging results to determine if surgical intervention is necessary and discuss the most appropriate treatment path for your unique situation.

What is the recovery time after brain tumour surgery?

Recovery time varies significantly depending on the size and location of the tumour, as well as the surgical approach used. Most patients can expect an initial hospital stay of several days, followed by a period of rest and gradual return to normal activities at home. Prof. Guive Sharifi will provide a personalized recovery timeline based on your specific procedure and overall health. While some patients may resume light activities within a few weeks, a full return to work or strenuous exercise may take longer, and your surgeon will monitor your progress closely throughout this time.

Does Prof. Guive Sharifi perform minimally invasive surgery for brain tumours?

Yes, Prof. Guive Sharifi is highly experienced in utilizing minimally invasive neurosurgical techniques whenever possible. These advanced methods are designed to reach brain tumours through smaller incisions, which helps to preserve healthy brain tissue and reduce the physical impact of the surgery. By using specialized instruments and high-definition imaging, he can perform complex procedures with greater precision. During your consultation, he will evaluate whether your specific tumour is suitable for a minimally invasive approach and explain the benefits and expectations associated with your particular surgical plan.

How can I prepare for my first consultation with a neurosurgeon?

To prepare for your first consultation, it is helpful to bring all relevant medical records, including recent MRI or CT scan reports and images. Write down a list of your current symptoms, how long you have experienced them, and any medications you are currently taking. Preparing a list of questions about the diagnosis, potential surgical risks, and recovery expectations can also help you make the most of your time with the surgeon. Prof. Guive Sharifi encourages open communication and will ensure that all your concerns are addressed during your visit to his clinic.
